Right car, right station, next 14 days

Project 4, Fleet Allocation Challenge  ·  Team The Bugfather (solo)  ·  SIXT Hackathon, 13 November 2026

The task

  • 10 stations, 4 car categories, 14 days, 2,357 cars.
  • Decide additions (455 each), removals (save 50–123 holding per day) and transfers by driver or truck (1–2 days, cars earn nothing in transit).
  • Future demand is unknown: estimate it from 8 weeks of rentals.
  • Scored on the mean profit over 30 hidden demand scenarios. An invalid plan ranks last.

What the data says

  • Airports peak Thursday, downtown peaks Saturday (1.44× the weekly mean). Every station follows its type's weekday shape.
  • Noise is Poisson with ~40 % extra variance (variance / mean 1.38), independent across stations, day-correlated within a station (rentals last 2.9 days).
  • Downtown rentals earn 7–9 % more per car-day than airport rentals, in every category.

Method: know, price the uncertainty, decide

One pipeline, everything per station × category × day; plans are judged by an independent re-implementation of the scoring rules, never by the solver's own objective

1 · Know and forecast

  • Level 0: committed cars, one-way moves, station fleet per day. All 10 check values match.
  • Forecast: cars on rent per weekday, shape pooled per station type, level per station. No trend (−0.3 %/week).
  • Bookings on file as a leading indicator: airports are booking 8–11 % above their usual pace (estimator-dependent), downtown is on pace. It did not backtest, so it is a stress case, not a bet.

2 · Price the uncertainty

  • Expected revenue of the k-th car at a station, averaged over 1,000 random futures (Poisson around the forecast).
  • Still a concave staircase of revenue "buckets", so the model does not grow, but the last cars are worth only the probability they get used.
  • Level 4 built into Levels 1–3. Beats naive "plan for 90 %" shading, which lost 25–40 k.

3 · Decide with one MILP

  • Adds, removals, transfers per station/category/day, trucks per route/day (PuLP + CBC).
  • Appendix A as constraints: fleet ≥ committed, caps, free capacity = max(0, util × fleet − committed), upgrades from the next category, 8 cars per truck.
  • Upgrade value = probability the higher category really has a free car at that station and day (≈1.0 premium→midsize at Marlow Airport, ≈0.3 midsize→compact).
  • Referee polish on top: edits kept only if unseen futures confirm them (+23 k).

Why a probability and not trust: trusting upgrades fully made the model move ~980 cars to exploit "free" premium cars that are not always free; ignoring upgrades threw revenue away. A 25-minute solve of the trusting model scored lower than a 10-minute one: the model was over-optimistic and a longer search exploits that.

What the plan does, and does it hold up?

28 cars added (21 economy, 7 compact), 220 removed (midsize and premium at their caps, economy one below), 751 transferred, 99 % by truck, transfer cost 90.5 k

The moves

  • Rebalance by truck in days 1–4: Marlow Airport 690 → 234 cars by day 14, Dunmere Airport 298 → 126, Fairgrounds 359 → 100; Marlow Central +173, Old Quarter +153, Quayside +133, Highvale +88, Lakeside +89.
  • One way, not a shuttle: 634 of 751 cars move in days 1–4 (452 airport → downtown, 298 downtown → downtown, 1 back), plus 99 cars on days 10–12 before the second Saturday. Airports keep enough for their own Mon–Thu demand. A truck place on the 3 km airport → centre route costs about 87, against a car-day worth 240–540.
  • Premium cars serve midsize demand instead of buying the 18 midsize cars the cap allows.
  • Downtown is left short on the two Saturdays (67 % and 80 % served, 90–100 % on other days): by the price factors the last third of a peak day earns only 0.4–0.5 of the base price, less than holding and moving a car for one day.

What did not work, limits, next steps

Rebuild: python solution/make_plan.py, then polish.py twice (Python 3.14, no API keys, data as shipped; time-limited solver, so another machine may return a slightly different model plan: the submitted one is plans/final.csv)

Did not work

  • Planning for 80–90 % of demand: −25 to −40 k. The revenue buckets already do this job, more precisely.
  • Booking pace as a forecast: no better than the weekday mean in a backtest. Kept as a stress case.
  • More solver time on an over-optimistic model: better for the model, worse for the scorer.
  • CBC with threads > 1 returned values that did not match its own solution; a warm start with only the integer decisions started from a hollow solution. Both fixed.

Limits

  • Profits above come from our re-implementation of Appendix A. The official feedback round scored the model plan at 4,096,594 and the polished plan at 4,112,935 on the public scenario, all cost lines matching to the cent.
  • The joint model stops at a time limit with a small gap (about 1 %).
  • Upgrade order in the model may differ slightly from the scorer's own-category-first rule.
  • The forecast has no booking dynamics beyond the level signal.

Next steps, and for SIXT

  • Model upgrade capacity as a random quantity instead of a probability weight; faster solver (HiGHS).
  • Validate the booking-pace signal on more history; if it holds, use it as a live level adjustment.
  • The same model can run every morning on the live snapshot. The expected-revenue buckets are what make it safe to act on a forecast that is known to be noisy.
